The challenge

Your challenge: take data that exists today and turn it into something a busy person can act on.

What we see every day

Timing matters

The same data means something different in January and in June. Answers go stale quickly, and a late or out-of-date answer can be worse than none.

Data is scattered

It lives across systems, spreadsheets and inboxes. Pulling it together, and trusting what you get, takes more effort than using it.

The users aren’t technical

The people who need the answers are experts in their own field, not developers. Whatever holds the data must be workable without help.

What great looks like

What to build: whatever form it takes, aim for these three qualities. The next slide covers how it is scored.

01

A clear idea

One sentence that says what it is. A well-argued concept with a rough prototype beats a polished build nobody can explain.

02

Honest about its limits

Say what it does well, where it breaks and what you would do with more time. Knowing the gaps counts for more than hiding them.

03

Usable by non-technical people

Someone with no technical background asks a plain-language question and gets a usable answer, with no query language and no training session.

Build whatever you like. Use live data: open Irish data (data.gov.ie, CSO), public calendars and registers, or a dataset you build yourselves. Keep it buildable in nine hours.

How it’s judged

How it is scored: four criteria, weighted equally.

Innovation

A fresh use of data, an unexpected combination of sources, or an insight nobody had before. A dashboard on its own will not score well here.

Impact

Would this matter to someone if it existed? Be clear about who benefits, how, and why it is worth doing. The bigger and more credible the difference, the better.

Technical depth

Sound engineering under the hood: real data, sensible transformations and a design you can explain. We will ask how it works, not just what it does.

Presentation and demo quality

Show the data driving the result live, and tell the story so that anyone in the room can follow it. Clarity beats polish.
